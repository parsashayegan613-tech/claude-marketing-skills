# Quality gate

Check the outline against each point. Fix failures before showing the outline.

## Does it deserve to exist?
- [ ] At least two information-gain items are built into sections (not just listed at the top),
      or the outline says plainly that the post will be generic and why.
- [ ] The chosen angle is visible in the title and changes how sections are ordered or framed.
      If you could swap in a competitor's name and nothing would change, the angle isn't working.
- [ ] There are 8 or fewer owner questions, each specific enough to send as a text message
      ("What do you usually charge for a 2-storey house?", not "Get pricing info"), and none of
      them is answered by the profile or is a decision you were asked to make yourself.

## Does it serve the reader?
- [ ] The intro answers the main question directly within its first two or three sentences,
      with an actual number when the question is about one (sourced market figure if needed).
- [ ] Each H2 has an opening answer, not just a topic label.
- [ ] Section order follows the reader's questions in the order they'd have them, most urgent
      first.
- [ ] Nothing important is left as "it depends" without saying what it depends on.
- [ ] Length estimate matches intent. A price question isn't stretched into a 2,500-word guide.

## Is it usable?
- [ ] The outline file is roughly 1,500–2,500 words. Check with `wc -w`. If it's longer, cut
      research notes and writer notes first.
- [ ] If the profile lists words to avoid, search the outline for them and their variants
      (`grep -i`) and rewrite any hits, including in social briefs and notes.

## Is it honest?
- [ ] No invented prices, statistics, reviews, certifications, offers or local facts. Unknowns
      are owner questions or cited to a source.
- [ ] Claims that need evidence (safety, health, savings) are flagged for checking.

## Will it convert?
- [ ] One clear CTA, specific to this reader, placed where they're ready and again at the end.
- [ ] At least one internal link goes to a service page.

## Generic-content smells (rewrite if you see them)
- Headings that could title any post on the topic ("Introduction", "Benefits of X", "Conclusion")
- "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to", "It's important to note", "Look no further"
- A section whose only purpose is a definition the reader already knows
- An FAQ that repeats questions the body already answered
- Social posts that just say "New blog post! Read more:"
