# Local service playbook

Read this when the business serves a geographic area (trades, home services, cleaning,
grooming, clinics, salons, auto, etc.).

## Why local posts are different

A local service post has two readers: the person with the problem, and the search engine trying
to decide whether this business is a credible local answer. Both want the same thing: evidence
that this business actually does this work, here. Generic advice anyone could write does nothing
for either. Specifics do.

## Topic patterns that work for local services

These are the recurring shapes. Use them to sanity-check the angle, not as a menu to fill.

| Pattern | Reader state | What the post must deliver |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost / price** ("how much does X cost in [city]") | Comparing, close to buying | A real local range in the first lines, what moves the price up or down, what's included, red flags in cheap quotes |
| **Signs you need X** | Problem-aware, unsure | Clear signs in plain language, which ones are urgent, what happens if you wait, when DIY is fine |
| **How often should I X** | Maintenance-minded | A straight answer, then how local conditions or situations change it (pets, allergies, home age, climate) |
| **DIY vs. pro** | Weighing effort vs. money | Honest about what DIY can do, where it goes wrong, the point where a pro is worth it |
| **What to expect / how it works** | About to book, nervous | Step-by-step of the real visit, time, mess, prep, what the customer needs to do |
| **Seasonal / timing** | Planning | When to book locally and why (weather, demand peaks, rebate deadlines) |
| **Comparison** (X vs. Y, method A vs. B) | Solution-aware | A clear recommendation for each situation, not "it depends" |
| **Local guide** (best dog parks in [area], [city] rules on X) | Not shopping yet | Genuinely useful local knowledge; builds local relevance and goodwill. Light CTA. |

## Local specifics to look for

- **Climate and season**: cold snaps, humidity, pollen, wildfire smoke, salt on roads, mud season
- **Housing stock**: typical home age, basements vs. slab, older ductwork, apartments vs. houses
- **Rules and money**: local codes, permits, HOA or condo rules, utility or government rebates
- **Demand patterns**: when the business is booked solid, when it's easy to get a slot
- **Neighbourhoods**: name a few where it's natural ("we see this a lot in older homes in X"),
  never a keyword list of every suburb

If you can't verify a local fact, make it an owner question (if it is worth one of the 8) or leave it out, rather than inventing it.
A wrong local claim costs more trust than a missing one.

## Prices

Price questions are the highest-intent local searches, and most local businesses dodge them.
Including a real range is often the single biggest advantage the post can have.

- Use the business's own range if known. Otherwise put the best sourced local market range in
  the intro (say where it's from and how old it is), make "your typical range" the first owner
  question, and give the writer the factors that change it.
- Never invent a precise price, and never present a market range as the business's own.
- Say what's included, and what makes a quote suspiciously cheap.

## Service area and location signals

- Mention the city or area naturally in the intro, one H2 where it fits, and the CTA.
- Link to the matching service page and, if it exists, the location page.
- Don't stuff: one natural mention beats ten forced ones, and stuffing reads as spam to people.

## Proof that works locally

- Photos from real local jobs (with permission) beat stock photos every time
- A short story: "A customer in [area] called because..." (anonymised)
- Review quotes that mention the specific service
- Licences, certifications, insurance, years serving the area

## Google Business Profile post

Derive one from the article: the single most useful point, a sentence of context, a CTA, and a
link to the post. GBP posts are short (aim for under 300 words, ideally ~100) and benefit from a
real photo.

## CTA for local services

Match the CTA to the reader's state: "Book your fall cleaning" for ready readers, "Not sure?
Send us a photo and we'll tell you if it can wait" for problem-aware ones. Include the mechanism
(phone number, booking link) the profile gives. Mention any real offer or guarantee, never an
invented one.
