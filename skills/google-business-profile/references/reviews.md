# Reviews

Reviews are the profile's second-biggest ranking factor in expert surveys (count, rating, and
especially recency), and they're what customers read before deciding. Replies are read by
future customers more than by the reviewer, so every reply is a small public ad for how the
business treats people.

## Replying

Read the review properly first: what happened, what the customer cares about, and whether it's
real.

**Every reply**
- Short: 2–4 sentences for positive reviews, 3–6 for negative ones.
- Specific to what they said. Mention the actual service or detail ("glad Biscuit came home
  without the burrs from the greenbelt"). No two replies should read the same.
- Signed by whoever will actually handle follow-up. If the owner is away, sign as the team
  ("— The Muddy Mutts team") rather than writing in the owner's voice.
- In the business's voice, without words it avoids.
- No keyword stuffing ("Thanks for choosing us for the best dog grooming in Austin!"). It reads
  as spam to the people you're trying to win.
- No sales pitch, no links. A phone number only in negative replies, as the way to reach someone
  about the complaint, and only the business's main line, never a personal cell.
- Never reveal private information or confirm details the reviewer didn't share.

**Positive (4–5 stars)**: thank them for something specific, add one human detail, and invite
them back if natural. Don't repeat the review back at them.

**Mixed (3 stars)**: thank them, acknowledge the specific issue plainly, say what you're doing or
did about it, and invite them to get in touch if it's unresolved.

**Negative (1–2 stars) from a real customer**
1. Stay calm and take responsibility for the experience without arguing about the details in
   public. ("I'm sorry your visit went this way" is fine; "you're wrong, it was matted" is not.)
2. Don't admit fault or liability on anything that could be a legal, injury, health or damage
   claim. Say you take it seriously and want to look into it directly.
3. Give a specific way to reach a named person offline.
4. If there's a real correction future readers need (for example the price was quoted in
   advance), state it once, calmly, without blaming the customer.
5. Flag it to the owner if it mentions injury, damage, discrimination, a legal threat or a refund
   demand. Those need a decision from them, and possibly their insurer, before or as well as a
   reply.

**Price complaints**: acknowledge, explain briefly what's included or what drives the price, and
don't apologise for charging fairly or offer a discount in public.

**Looks fake** (no matching customer, wrong business, competitor, spam, off-topic): tell the owner
how to report it (from the profile: the review's menu → Report review; or Google's review
management tool) and which policy it breaks. Still write a short, calm reply in case the report
fails, which says you can't find a record of the visit and invites them to get in touch. Don't
accuse them of lying.

**Mark what you don't know.** If a reply needs a fact you don't have (whether the dog was
actually injured, what the customer was quoted), write it with a clear `[CHECK: ...]` blank and
put the question in the owner list.

## Asking for reviews

The fix for a review gap is a steady, simple habit: ask every customer, right after the job,
with a direct link. Most businesses that "can't get reviews" just don't ask consistently.

- **Ask everyone the same way.** No screening first, no "only send to happy customers", and no
  conditional wording in the request itself ("If you were happy with us, please..."). Ask for
  honest feedback instead.
- **No incentives** of any kind. If the user suggests a discount or freebie for reviews, say no
  clearly, explain the risk (reviews removed, profile restricted, and in some places a consumer-law
  issue), and give the compliant version.
- **Don't ask customers to name staff**, and don't set per-employee review targets (reported 2026
  policy).
- **The customer's own device.** Send a link by text or email; don't hand them a tablet.
- **Direct link.** Use the profile's "Ask for reviews" / share review form link (in the profile
  menu); write it as `[REVIEW LINK]` if you don't have it.
- **Timing.** Within a day of the job, ideally the same day, when they're happiest.

Templates to provide (adapt to the business voice):
- **Text message** (under 300 characters, one link, from a real person's name)
- **Email** (short, subject line, one link)
- **What to say in person** at the end of a job (one or two sentences)
- Optionally a follow-up after 5–7 days for people who didn't respond (once only)

```markdown
# Reviews: [Business] — [date]

## Replies
### [Reviewer] — [stars] — [one-line summary]
**Reply** ([n] characters):
[reply]
**Note for owner**: [only if needed: flag, report steps, CHECK items]

## Review requests
[templates]

## Not recommended
[anything the user asked for that breaks policy, and the alternative]

## Questions for the owner (max 6)
```
