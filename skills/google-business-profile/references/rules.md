# Google Business Profile rules (as of November 2026)

Google changes GBP often. If you can browse, check anything that matters against Google's
"Guidelines for representing your business on Google" (support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)
and the review policy pages. Items marked **(reported)** come from local SEO practitioners rather
than Google's own pages: follow them because the downside is real, but say they're reported if a
user asks.

## Fields and limits

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Business name** | The real-world name as it appears on signage, website and invoices. No keywords, cities, taglines, phone numbers or "Best". Legal suffixes (LLC, Inc) only if they're used in the real name. Keyword-stuffed names are the most common suspension trigger. |
| **Primary category** | Pick the most specific category for the core business: "this business IS a ___". It's the strongest profile ranking factor in expert surveys. |
| **Secondary categories** | As few as needed, each one something the business *is*. Not keywords and not things it merely *has*. |
| **Description** | 750 characters max. Describe services, who you serve, history, what's different. No prices, promotions, sales, URLs or links, and no keyword lists. Google shows roughly the first 250 characters before "More", so lead with the most important line. |
| **Address / service area** | Real physical address only (no P.O. boxes or virtual offices). Service-area businesses that don't serve customers at their address should hide it and list the areas they serve. Google says the service area shouldn't extend beyond about **2 hours' driving** from the base. |
| **Hours** | Real customer-facing hours. Set special hours for holidays. Appointment-only businesses may leave hours off. Being open at search time is a reported ranking factor. |
| **Phone** | A number that reaches this location and is controlled by the business. Local numbers preferred. |
| **Website link** | The business's own page. For multi-service or multi-location businesses, link the matching location or service page. |
| **Services** | List real services with short descriptions (about 300 characters each). Remove any services Google auto-added that the business doesn't offer **(reported suspension risk)**. |
| **Posts** | Up to 1,500 characters. Types: Update, Offer, Event. Each can have a photo and a button (Book, Order, Learn more, Sign up, Call). Phone numbers in the post text often get the post rejected **(reported)**; use the Call button instead. Posts don't have a confirmed ranking effect, but they show the business is active and convert viewers. |
| **Photos** | Real photos of the team, vehicles, work and premises. No stock images, no text-heavy graphics as the main photos. Recent photos matter; Google now sorts by most recent **(reported)**. |
| **Review replies** | Up to 4,096 characters, but short is better. Replies are public and permanent; treat them as advertising read by future customers. |

## Gone or changed (don't recommend the old way)

- **Q&A is gone.** Google shut the Q&A API on 3 November 2025 and phased the public Q&A section
  out of profiles after that. Don't tell anyone to "seed your Q&A". Instead, put the answers to
  common questions on the profile itself (description, services, attributes) and on the
  website.
- **Ask Maps** (launched March 2026) is Google's AI assistant in Maps that answers questions
  about a business. It reportedly draws on the profile, reviews, photos and website. The way to
  influence it is to make the profile and website say the same things clearly: services, areas,
  hours, what you don't do, parking, accessibility, booking. A useful audit step is to ask Ask
  Maps three or four real customer questions and fix the source of any wrong answer.
- **Messaging/chat** inside GBP ended in July 2024. A WhatsApp button is available in some
  regions **(reported, 2026)**.
- **Insights** became **Performance** (verified profiles only).

## Reviews policy

These are the rules most small businesses break without knowing it:

- **No incentives.** No discounts, freebies, raffle entries or donations in exchange for a review,
  even "for an honest review". Violations can get reviews removed and the profile restricted,
  and in some countries it's a consumer-law offence.
- **No review gating.** Don't ask only happy customers, and don't screen customers first ("Were you
  happy? Yes → Google, No → tell us"). Ask everyone the same way.
- **No staff-name asks or quotas (reported, April 2026).** Don't ask customers to mention a staff
  member by name, and don't run per-employee review quotas.
- **No kiosks or shared devices (reported).** Reviews should come from the customer's own phone or
  computer, not a tablet at the counter.
- **No reviews from owners, staff or family**, and no buying reviews or swapping them with other
  businesses.
- **Fake or policy-breaking reviews** (spam, conflict of interest, off-topic, hate, personal info)
  can be reported through the profile ("Report review") or Google's review management tool.
  Owners cannot delete reviews. A 1-star from a real but unhappy customer is not a policy
  violation.
- Google announced in April 2026 that it detects review-spam spikes, removes them, may pause new
  reviews, and shows a banner to customers. Anything that looks like a review campaign can trip
  this, which is another reason to ask steadily rather than in bursts.

## Privacy in replies

Never confirm someone was a customer beyond what they've said themselves, and never post
details of their visit, health, pet's health, address, invoice or phone number. This matters
most for clinics (medical, dental, vet): even "we reviewed your file" can be a breach.
