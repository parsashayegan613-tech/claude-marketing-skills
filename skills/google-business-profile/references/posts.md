# Posts

GBP posts show up on the profile in Search and Maps. They don't have a confirmed ranking effect,
but a profile with recent, useful posts looks open and active, and a good post answers the
question someone has right before they call. Write them for the customer looking at the profile,
not for an algorithm.

## Plan the month

- **Rhythm**: one post a week is a good default for a small business; 4–5 posts per month.
- **Mix**: mostly Updates (useful tips, recent jobs, seasonal reminders, team/van/behind the
  scenes), plus an Offer only if a real offer exists, plus an Event only if there's a real event.
- **Timing**: tie posts to what's happening locally that month: weather, holidays, school terms,
  the business's busy season, local events. Note any holiday hours the profile should show.
- **Sources**: the business's blog posts, common customer questions, recent jobs, reviews
  (paraphrase; quote a review only if the profile says you have permission), real offers.

## Each post

```markdown
### Post [n] — [publish date] — [Update / Offer / Event]
**Photo**: [what to shoot or pick: a real photo, and what should be in it]
**Text** ([n] characters):
[the post]
**Button**: [Book / Call / Learn more / Order / Sign up] → [where it goes]
[Offer only: title, start/end dates, terms. Event only: title, start/end date and time.]
```

Writing the text:
- **First line is the hook.** Only the first line or two show before "more". Lead with the
  problem or the useful point ("Frozen pipe? Do this before you call anyone."), not the business
  name.
- **One idea per post, kept short**: aim for about 300–700 characters (60–130 words). Google
  allows 1,500, but most people only read the first lines, and a long post buries the point.
- **Vary the button** to fit the post: Book when there's online booking, Learn more when the
  post links to a blog article or service page, Call for urgent problems. Five Call buttons in
  a row reads as a sales feed.
- **Useful first, sales second.** Give the tip or the answer, then a one-line reason to book.
- **Local, naturally.** Mention the city or a neighbourhood where it fits. Don't list suburbs.
- **No phone numbers in the text**, no ALL CAPS, no "click here", no excessive emojis. Use the
  button for the call to action. Posts with phone numbers are often rejected.
- **No invented offers or prices.** If you don't know of a real offer, don't write an Offer post.
  If you mention a price, it must come from the profile or the user.
- **Respect the voice and words to avoid** from the client profile.

## File layout

```markdown
# GBP posts: [Business] — [Month Year]

## This month
[2–3 lines: the seasonal context and the plan]
**Holiday hours to set**: [dates, or "none"]

[Posts 1–5]

## Photos needed
[checklist of the photos the owner needs to take or find]

## Questions for the owner (max 6) / Assumptions
```

Count characters with a script, not by eye.
